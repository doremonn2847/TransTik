# Playbook: virality explainer

Goal: a qualitative explanation of why a video may have performed the way it did. This is an
informed reading of the transcript and public numbers, not a measurement and not a prediction.
Say so in one sentence at the start.

## Gather
Call `get_transcript` with timestamps and stats. Use what is present; say what is missing.
Available numbers may include views, likes, comments, shares, follower count, duration, post time,
and the ratios `likes_per_view`, `comments_per_view`, `shares_per_view`.

Rough reading of ratios (they vary by niche and platform, so treat them as hints):
- likes per view above about 0.08 suggests strong approval; below 0.02 suggests passive viewing.
- comments per view above about 0.005 suggests the video provoked opinions or questions.
- shares per view above about 0.01 suggests the content is useful or identity-signalling.
- views far above follower count mean the reach came from recommendation, not the existing audience.

## Analyze
1. **Hook strength**: quote the first line; what expectation does it create, and how fast?
2. **Retention structure**: where are the open loops, pattern breaks and payoffs? Any dead stretch?
3. **Emotional triggers**: curiosity, surprise, anger, belonging, aspiration, humour; cite the line.
4. **Clarity and density**: words per minute, jargon, one idea or several?
5. **CTA**: present, placement, and whether it fits the comment/share numbers.
6. **What the numbers support**: connect each claim to a ratio where one exists.

## Limits to state plainly
Without retention curves, watch time, sound/visual analysis or posting-context data, causes cannot
be proven. The visuals, editing, music and the creator's existing audience are invisible to a
transcript and may matter more than the words.

## Output
A short verdict paragraph, the six sections above in brief, then three specific, transferable
lessons the user could apply to their own next video.
